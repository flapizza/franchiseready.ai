# Tasks and Follow-Up Intelligence

`ConsultantTask` is the canonical accepted-work aggregate. It has a stable `taskId`, consultant ownership, optional candidate/brand/referral links, an explicit `open | completed | cancelled` status, understandable `low | normal | high | urgent` priority, due/completion timestamps, and immutable provenance. `createdByConsultantId` and `assignedToConsultantId` preserve the future team-assignment boundary without introducing delegation UI.

Next Best Actions and follow-up recommendations are advisory evidence, not tasks. Existing email engagement, Discovery, Brand Presentation, Referral Studio, and lifecycle engines remain authoritative for their own evidence. `FollowUpRecommendationService` adapts their outputs into deterministic recommendation read models. A consultant may accept a recommendation into exactly one task, choose a due date, dismiss it, or ignore it. Acceptance preserves the recommendation ID and reason; dismissal is demo-persisted. Snooze is intentionally deferred.

`TaskRepository` is persistence-neutral. `DemoTaskRepository` combines believable seed tasks with the existing process-local demo overlay. `TaskService` owns creation, editing, completion, cancellation, reopening, ownership checks, candidate validation, duplicate prevention, and meaningful candidate activity events. React uses authenticated server actions and never advances candidate lifecycle when a task changes.

`TaskRuntime` derives overdue and consultant-local Today state rather than persisting redundant flags. It also owns operational sorting: overdue urgent/high, today urgent/high, other today work, upcoming work, then history; due time and explicit priority break ties. Complete serializable read models feed Tasks, Candidate 360, CRM, and Mission Control, keeping date behavior and hydration deterministic.

Tasks are operational commitments. Candidate priority remains opportunity/risk intelligence and is a separate type. Candidate 360 shows concise linked work; Mission Control surfaces overdue/today/high-priority accepted work beside clearly labeled recommendations; CRM only exposes useful open-task context. Custom pipeline labels are presentation data and never drive recommendation rules—canonical lifecycle and evidence do.

Future boundaries remain separate: calendar events and meetings coexist with task due dates but are not tasks; notification channels may subscribe to overdue/important tasks; stage/email/referral events may later feed a configurable automation engine; recurring tasks require their own recurrence model; analytics may derive creation, completion, overdue rate, completion time, follow-up delay, and conversion relationships from retained history. No calendar, notification, automation, recurrence, or analytics engine is implemented in this feature.
